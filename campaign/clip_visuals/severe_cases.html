Drainage-clip severe cases — diagnostic sheet (rewired clip, commit 59e6a12)

The 5 cases where the rewired clip (target = outline's lower 50% by elevation, candidates no longer auto-pass) removes more than 50% of the release band, out of 105 real cases (84 no-ops, 21 clipped, 5 severe, 0 emptied). aval_8025, aval_11149, aval_13404 were dumped by the real calibrate dump CLI (not refused). aval_8124 and aval_13722 are refused outright (kept cells fall below the 20-cell floor) so calibrate dump produces nothing for them; their rasters come from a standalone probe that reuses the real data_processor/compute_core library code for DEM fetch + outline rasterisation and a verbatim copy of build_release's clip logic from the current calibrate.rs -- cross-validated pixel-for-pixel against the real dump output on the three non-refused cases before being trusted for the two refused ones.

Overall read: 4 of 5 look like genuine ridge/watershed leaks; the biggest one (aval_13722, 97.8% removed) looks like a false positive.

For aval_8025, aval_8124, aval_11149 and aval_13404, sampled removed cells' D8 descent paths travel 335-735 m (67-147 steps) and land either at a real interior DEM pit clearly outside the observed polygon's own bounding box, or at the padded domain's edge column after crossing the whole domain -- a large, unambiguous departure from the mapped footprint in either case. That's the failure mode the clip was built for.

aval_13722 is different in kind: removed cells travel only 80-230 m before all converging on one real but small interior pit (every neighbour of (103,110) is higher) that sits close to the release, not a long-distance leak. Its outline is also atypical -- a ~600 m ribbon at roughly constant elevation with the release confined to one tip, so the elevation-defined "lower body" target lands far along the ribbon regardless of real hydrological connectivity. A mass flow with momentum would plausibly cross a shallow local hollow like this; pure D8 cannot. Recommend human review of this one before trusting the refusal.
