§1 · Method

How anything here was decided

The end goal of this project is P(runout | conditions): given weather, snowpack, and terrain, a probability distribution over avalanche runout footprints for Tirol. The architecture splits the problem in two — a GPU simulator with frozen general physics renders a footprint from a small parameter vector, and a separate conditions model predicts a distribution over the few knobs that genuinely vary event to event. This campaign is the freeze: measuring which constants are general, and what their values should be.

The measurement design is a staged profile search. For every candidate value of a global constant, the per-event nuisance parameters (μ, slab depth) are re-fit from scratch on each of the panel's ~105 avalanches, and candidates are compared on the score they reach after that re-fit. This is profile likelihood: a global candidate never gets to look bad merely because the nuisances were tuned for its rival. Comparisons are paired per-case (each avalanche is its own control), with bootstrap confidence intervals, and a pre-registered decision band: a challenger must beat the incumbent by ΔΩT ≥ 0.02 with a CI excluding zero, otherwise the incumbent stands. A full narrative of why this beats both grid search and per-case-averaging is in campaign/search_algorithm.html.

The score, ΩT = 2·IoU − 1, compares the simulated footprint against the mapped avalanche outline: +1 is a perfect match, 0 is the coin-flip boundary, negative means mostly wrong. One framing rule governs every number in this report: mapped outlines themselves only agree between human mappers at about ΩT ≈ +0.28 (Hafner et al. 2023). That is the reproducibility ceiling of the ground truth. Absolute scores are read against that ceiling, never against 1.0 — and every claim in this campaign rests on paired contrasts, which stay valid below the ceiling.

For scale: with per-event nuisances fitted at the frozen structure the panel reaches a mean ΩT around 0.32 — at the mapping noise floor. The same panel evaluated at repo-default parameters with no per-event fitting scores 0.005. The gap between those two numbers is the entire reason the harvest (per-event calibration at scale) exists.

§2 · Stage −1

Fixing the instrument before trusting it

Two defects were visible in preliminary results — both spotted by eye, both confirmed real, both fixed and measured before any calibration stage ran. Calibrating against a scoring function with known artifacts would have baked those artifacts into every frozen constant.

Single-particle tails

Isolated particles ran on far past the physically plausible runout, dragging the footprint's reach with them. The fix is a residence gate: a cell only counts as affected if enough material actually dwelt there. Raw residence counts scale with both particle count and timestep, so the gate is normalised — count · cfl / ppc ≥ 0.25 — making it comparable across numerics settings (essential, because cfl and ppc were themselves being chosen in stage 0; an un-normalised gate would have been circular). The 0.25 threshold was swept, not guessed: over {0, 0.125, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4} it sits on the plateau, paired +0.0029 against no gate (CI +0.0022…+0.0036, better on 82/105 cases). On the final panel it trims a mean of ~11 m of spurious reach per case.

The ridge leak

Mapped release polygons don't align perfectly with the DEM. Where a polygon crests a ridge, part of the release drops into the neighbouring watershed — mass simulated on the wrong side of the mountain. The fix traces D8 steepest-descent from every release cell and clips cells whose descent path never reaches the observed outline's lower body. On the panel: 18 of 104 cases are touched, losing on average 20% of their release cells; 4 are severe (≥50% clipped, kept but flagged); one case (aval_8124) falls below the 20-release-cell floor and is refused outright rather than scored on a sliver.

The tautology trap

The first implementation of the clip was a silent no-op: it tested descent into the full outline polygon, and since release cells are inside the polygon by construction, every cell trivially passed — clipped fraction 0.0 on 105/105 cases. It was caught only because the validation ran the real binary and rendered before/after images for eyeballs, per the user's request. The fix targets the outline's lower body instead, and an end-to-end regression test now runs red on the old code. Two further traps followed: aval_13722 was falsely refused because D8 descent died in a 2 m terrain hollow (fixed with priority-flood pit filling, 2 m tolerance), and aval_8075 was a third false positive sitting below the severity threshold — invisible to eyeball review, caught only by the numbers.

Per-case before/after renders for every severe case, with the D8 traces, are committed at campaign/clip_visuals/severe_cases.html. The full validation table is campaign/results_2026-07-28/fix_validation.json.

Stage 0 · numerics · 75 grid points, per-case re-fit at each

Numerics: the surface is not monotone

Numerics parameters — CFL number, particles per cell, step cap — are supposed to be boring: finer should be asymptotically better, and you pick the cheapest setting statistically equivalent to the finest. That rule failed here, and the failure is the finding.

Because ΩT is non-monotone in resolution, "equivalent to the finest setting" is not a valid anchor — the finest setting isn't the best one. The campaign nearly adopted cfl 0.35 / ppc 16 by mechanically applying the equivalence rule; re-reading the response surface showed the repo default cfl 0.5 / ppc 8 / max_steps 3000 beats it by +0.0118 at 1.9× lower cost, and it froze instead. Cheaper settings were rejected on robustness rather than mean score: max_steps 1500 binds against real long-running cases, and ppc 4's variance is qualitatively worse even where its mean survives.

Lesson, recorded

An instrument's own output does not validate the instrument. The near-mistake was caught by reproducing the recommendation against the full response surface instead of trusting the decision rule that produced it. This is also why a pre-harvest gate now exists: stage 0 validated numerics at per-case optima, but the harvest spends most of its evaluations off-optimum where runs lengthen and the step cap binds — the ordering must be re-confirmed there before the harvest runs (tracked in automated_suggestions_todo.md).

The likely mechanism for non-monotonicity — mapped outlines are themselves generous, and coarser dynamics diffuse footprints slightly outward toward that generosity — is a hypothesis for Markus, not a measured claim (notes_for_markus.md item 24).

Stage 1 · model structure · 48 candidates · 163,177 evaluations

Structure: samosAT wins, decisively

Four friction models × twelve physics-flag combinations (curvature, particle interaction, earth pressure, entrainment), every candidate given its own full per-case re-fit. The result is the campaign's biggest single decision: samosAT friction with curvature, particle interaction, and earth pressure on, entrainment off (m3_samosat_c1p1e1n0) replaces the Voellmy incumbent, +0.036 on the headline metric (p ≈ 6×10−6), and the win holds under all four defensible metric readings (+0.033 to +0.079).

Two structural facts came out of the same data:

  • Entrainment is inert in this configuration. Toggling the flag produces bit-identical raw footprints (top pair in the chart differs only by run noise in the re-fit). The likely cause is that no erodible-depth field is ever supplied to the solver. This is not a claim that entrainment physics doesn't matter — it's a claim that the flag currently does nothing, and the 1999 wet-snow track (where entrainment has a real physical job) must resolve the contradiction before trusting it (notes_for_markus.md item 8).
  • μ changed meaning tonight. Fitted μ under samosAT is not comparable to Voellmy μ. Every μ-based correlation in earlier project notes (terrain r = +0.42, the noise-ceiling R², the aval_shape leak) was measured against Voellmy fits and must be re-measured against samosAT-μ targets before the conditions model trains on them.
Stage 2 · drag coefficient ξ · skipped, by measurement

ξ exits the model

Stage 2 was designed to profile the Voellmy drag coefficient ξ. It never ran, for the best possible reason: samosAT reads no drag coefficient — ξ is not a parameter of the winning structure. This removes what had been a persistently non-identified direction in earlier fits (μ and ξ trading off against each other along a ridge). The harvest inner loop is now a clean two-parameter problem, (μ, slab), with no degenerate direction. The Voellmy finalist carried into stage 4 keeps its own fitted ξ = 754 for a fair comparison.

Stage 3 · internal friction angle · 24,129 evaluations

ifa: a validity edge, not a peak

The internal friction angle enters only through the earth-pressure term, and the design predicted a flat profile. The measurement said otherwise — 15–30° are significantly worse, 35–45° is a statistical plateau — and chasing down why produced the most instructive mechanism of the campaign.

The mechanism sits in one line of shader code. The earth-pressure coefficient computes

inside = 1.0 − cos²(ifa) / cos²(basal_friction_angle)   // grid_physics.wgsl:151
root   = sqrt(max(inside, 0.0))

inside > 0 requires ifa > basal_friction_angle, which is hard-coded at 25°. Below that, the max(…, 0) clamp silently zeroes the term and the earth-pressure coefficient loses its dependence on the flow state entirely. This is the classical Rankine / Savage–Hutter validity condition — internal friction must exceed basal friction for active/passive states to exist — and the arithmetic matches the measured profile exactly: 25° gives inside = 0 precisely, 30° a small root (0.295), 40° a healthy 0.535.

So the correct claim is not "40° is optimal". It is: 40° sits comfortably inside the physically valid region, and anything in 35–45° is statistically equivalent — a weaker statement about ifa and a stronger one about the freeze's robustness.

A pre-registered confound check also closed: ifa and density are both resistance-like, so ifa's optimum might have been compensating for the frozen density. Measured at density 150 / 200 / 300, the optimum is 40° at every density with a stable profile shape — a clean null. Mechanically sensible: density scales basal friction while ifa scales lateral earth pressure; unlike μ and density, they aren't competing for the same job.

Why density is frozen at 200 at all

Under Voellmy, density cancels analytically. Under samosAT it is live (a monotone 0.0124 swing) — but via a shader inconsistency: compute_particles.wgsl hard-codes const density = 200.0 in one place while using the settings value in mass_per_area, so the settings density effectively acts as a friction multiplier. Fitting it would fit a bug and recreate a μ-degenerate direction. It is frozen at 200 (the shader's own constant), and the inconsistency is filed upstream for Markus (notes_for_markus.md item 25).

Addendum, the morning after — the user called me (Claude, the agent that wrote this report) a dunce, and was right to

Reviewing the paragraph above, the user asked the obvious question this report had talked itself around: if the bug is one code site ignoring the settings value, why is the resolution a frozen constant and a politely filed note — rather than the world's simplest bug fix, deleting the hard-coded number and reading the setting? The defense offered ("the fix direction is ambiguous without knowing what the reference formulation intends") was, on inspection, the same shape of argument this campaign catalogued seven failures of in its own honesty section: an assumption standing in for a check that costs nothing. Dunce, as charged.

So the reference was read instead of guessed at. The AvaFrame samosAT formulation defines R_s = ρ·ū²/σb with one density used consistently — and since the basal normal stress σb is itself proportional to ρ, the ratio is density-free by construction, a Froude-type number. There is no reading in which the hard-coded numerator is intentional; the shader itself even contains a correctly-built settings-density rs that is computed and then never used — the fingerprint of an unfinished refactor. The one-word fix is applied at commit d4cbb3e: arithmetically identical at the frozen density = 200, so every number in this report stands unchanged, and at every other density the knob is now inert instead of a spurious friction multiplier — density exits samosAT dynamics entirely, the same way ξ did. The measured 0.0124 swing was pure bug. Empirical verification (a pipeline smoke test on a Vulkan box and a 100/200/300 identity check) is queued for the next box session; the reasoning above is exact arithmetic, but this campaign has earned its skepticism of exact arguments, including this one.

§3 · audit

The hidden-constant audit

The ifa mechanism exposed a blind spot: a constant that isn't in the harness's parameter struct is invisible to this entire design — every stage is defined over exposed parameters. So every SimSettings field not exposed was audited against what the shaders actually read:

constantdefaultread by the frozen model?status
basal_friction_angle25.0°yes — with ifa, into the earth-pressure coefficient; defines the ifa validity boundarylive, never swept
velocity_threshold1e-6yes — raw at two sites (particle-stop and no-friction cutoffs); floored to 1e-3 at a thirdmeasured: inert
n070.0no — its only use is commented outunreachable
i0, mu0, mu2, grain_diameter—no — μ(I) branch only (model 5, unused)unreachable

basal_friction_angle sat at its default through every stage, so nothing measured is invalidated — but the frozen vector records it explicitly as fixed at default, never swept, rather than letting a reader assume it was considered. It co-determines the earth-pressure coefficient and the valid ifa range, and it is the prime suspect for the 1999 wet-snow track, where basal conditions are exactly what changes.

velocity_threshold: measured, inert. Because "almost certainly inert" is the same shape of argument that was wrong twice tonight, the constant was exposed as a parameter (commit 0fe8fc1) and measured: a two-point check (1e-6 vs 1e-3) across the full 105-case panel at the frozen vector. Result: mean ΩT identical to four decimals, 94 of 104 scored cases bit-identical, the remaining 10 differing by at most 0.0053 — inside the solver’s documented atomic-summation noise and two orders of magnitude under the decision band. The default freezes as verified inert, not assumed inert.

Stage 4 · confirmation · 41,653 evaluations · 180/180 jobs clean

Confirmation: attacking the campaign's weakest assumption

Every ranking above rests on one assumption: that the single-start inner-loop re-fit found scores good enough to rank candidates fairly. Stage 4 attacks exactly that. The four finalists — three samosAT flag variants and the Voellmy incumbent at its fitted ξ — were each re-run three times from deliberately different inner-loop starting points. Four pass criteria, pre-registered: (1) the winner keeps rank against all three alternates; (2) its margin over Voellmy holds with a CI excluding zero; (3) calibration and validation splits agree in sign; (4) pooling three starts lifts scores by less than the 0.02 decision band — because a bigger lift would mean stage 1's single-start rankings were search-budget artifacts.

This is the same pathology seen earlier in the campaign — Voellmy inner loops converge far less reliably than samosAT's.

Why the freeze survives this: the headline +0.0207 margin is computed inside stage 4, where both sides got the identical three-start budget — the winner beats the best version of the incumbent that equalised search can produce, which is precisely the comparison criterion 4 exists to protect. The residual risk — that some non-finalist was understated in stage 1 badly enough to leapfrog — would require a mean lift of roughly +0.035, larger than anything measured for any candidate, delivered uniformly rather than by the few-stuck-cases mechanism actually observed. The design authority closed it by measurement rather than judgment: ranking all 48 stage-1 candidates against the winner’s confirmed score, exactly two non-finalists sit within the largest lift ever observed — and both are the entrainment-ON twins of finalists already in stage 4, proven bit-identical simulations (raw footprints equal on 101/101 cases). They cannot leapfrog the winner; they are the winner. The nearest genuinely distinct candidate would need +0.0306, exceeding the largest observed lift by 20%, delivered uniformly rather than by the few-stuck-cases mechanism actually seen. One correction was recorded into the plan for future reuse: the criterion as originally written tested the wrong quantity (the winner’s own lift, which is irrelevant if it still leads under equalised budget) — the operative test is whether any non-finalist sits within the maximum observed lift of the winner’s confirmed score. Verdict: all four criteria pass; vector v1 frozen.

§4 · cross-dataset transfer

Do the globals apply to all the datasets?

The freeze was measured on the 2018 storm panel. The project holds three mapped-outline datasets — 2018 (602 clean cases), 2019 (443), 1999 (457), extracted through an identical, verified funnel — and a frozen vector is only "general" if it survives contact with the other two. This is a measurement, not a gate: the global vector freezes on stage 4 alone, and a dataset that rejects the frozen structure becomes a finding for the per-dataset track, not a rollback.

Design: a seeded ~100-case random panel from each pool; on each panel, the four stage-4 finalists (does the structure ranking transfer?) and a four-point ifa profile (does the validity-edge shape transfer?); plus the pooled union for a single "global-global" read. 2019 shares the dry-storm regime with 2018, so it isolates "different storm" as a single factor; 1999 contains the wet/full-depth suspects, where the design already predicts structure selection may need to re-run from scratch — a transfer failure there is expected to be informative, not alarming.

Panel soundness first

Every panel passed the screen before its rankings were read. The campaign control reproduced its own known behaviour exactly (same single refusal, same four severe cases at identical percentages — the instrument is stable). 2018-holdout and 2019 show clip statistics indistinguishable from the control. 1999 first hit a data bug, not a physics problem: its pool writes explicit JSON nulls for its dead source columns, which the harness's defaulting didn't cover — all 100 cases refused to parse. After the fix (data-side that night, null-tolerant deserialization committed permanently), 1999 screened at 99/100 — and its clip-fraction distribution turned out to be the cleanest of the four panels (scored mean 0.018 vs 0.036–0.039 elsewhere; zero scored cases past the 50% severity line, where the others have 1–4). That is itself a result: the scoring conventions transfer across mapping standards — a dataset with 91% quality-2 outlines from a different era and filter chain registers against its DEMs at least as well as 2018's 25%-quality-1 panel, evidence the conventions are instrument properties rather than artifacts tuned to one panel.

The step at 25° travels — and this is arguably the strongest result of the campaign. These are single fixed-(μ, slab) runs — no inner loop, hence the much lower absolute values — and in every panel ifa 25° is the worst point, with the step off it 1.5–2.6× larger than the entire spread across the 30/40/45 plateau, whose width is constant to a thousandth across four independent datasets (0.0054–0.0064). It is the only finding of the night that was predicted from reading the source — the max(inside, 0) clamp — then confirmed on its own panel, then reproduced across different storms, mapping standards, and trigger populations. A fitted artifact does not reproduce like that; everything else in this campaign is a comparison, this is a mechanism. It also sharpens the audit flag above: basal_friction_angle sets this boundary identically on every dataset we have, globally live, and it has never been swept.

What 1999 can and cannot say

On the 1999 panel the earth-pressure-off variant has the highest point estimate, 0.0035 above the frozen variant. The difference is not statistically distinguishable, and it is smaller than the amount by which this same contrast varies between two independent draws of the 2018 dataset. Earth pressure is a plausible place for wet-snow behaviour to differ — but nothing here supports that reading: the 1999 panel is confounded across flow regime, storm, mapping standard and trigger mix simultaneously, and its extraction carries no humidity attribute at all, so the wet cases cannot even be identified. More pointedly, the one mechanism-level measurement available cuts against it: the ifa profile below shows the earth-pressure formulation behaving identically on 1999 — the same validity edge at 25°, a plateau of the same width — so there is no measured sense in which earth pressure differs on that dataset. There is also a parsimonious non-physics alternative: EP-off is the arm that flipped cal/val sign in stage 4, and an arm with higher subgroup variance will occasionally top a panel by chance. The observation is recorded as a hypothesis for the per-dataset track, not as evidence.

One more caveat the transfer data itself teaches: absolute ΩT is not comparable across panels. 1999 posts the highest raw scores of the four despite 91% quality-2 outlines; four candidate explanations — avalanche size mix (wrong sign), digitisation smoothness (identical across panels), outline quality class (direction flips between panels), conservative mapping (same-class footprints are the same size) — were each tested against the data and each rejected. The rejected list travels with the anomaly so the next person knows which four guesses not to spend time on. Every panel has its own achievable ceiling, set by factors this campaign demonstrated it cannot decompose — which is exactly why the transfer design rests on within-panel paired contrasts, and why the curiosity changes nothing about the reading. A deeper limitation from the same audit: 1999 has zero coverage on every deployable feature (start zone, aspect, fracture width), so it can supply calibration targets but can never test the conditions model's central bet. 2019 is the only new dataset that can.

§5 · operations

Costs, fleet, and operations

The campaign ran on three rented VAST.ai boxes — cheap cards, deliberately: the workload saturates on job-submission overhead at ~23 cases/s per card regardless of GPU class, so economics favour many slow cards (~$1.70 per million simulations on 4070-class vs ~$4 on 5090-class). GPU count is a latency decision, not a cost decision.

All three boxes were destroyed the same night the campaign ended, after every artifact was verified home twice over: the structured sweep (213 MB, 4,531 files, integrity-checked) plus complete raw tarballs of each box’s data directory (A: 1,581 files, B: 5,995, C: 3,014 — all listing clean before anything was destroyed). Teardown was verified by both runbook methods — the instance list returning none of ours, and SSH refusing on all three endpoints. Total fleet cost for the entire campaign, at ~$1.32/hr across three boxes for roughly one working day: on the order of $15–25 — less than the price of the coffee consumed thinking about it.

Everything operational learned tonight — the Vulkan/GLVND package trap on CUDA images, the dead-host container failure mode, verified teardown, the kill-by-PID discipline, why listings must be re-verified after provisioning — is written into the operations runbook so none of it has to be rediscovered.

§6 · honesty section

Caveats, debts, and what happens next

Standing caveats

  • The reproducibility ceiling. Ground truth agrees with itself at ΩT ≈ +0.28. Per-event calibrated scores sit at that floor already; absolute-score improvements beyond it are chasing mapping noise. All claims here are paired contrasts, which survive this — but any future reader quoting absolute ΩT must quote the ceiling with it.
  • Entrainment's contradiction is unresolved. The flag is inert (bit-identical footprints), most plausibly for want of an erodible-depth input. Must be resolved before the 1999 wet track, where entrainment has a real job.
  • basal_friction_angle = 25° was never swept. Explicitly fixed-at-default. Tier-2 candidate for per-dataset work; prime suspect for wet snow.
  • μ-semantics break. All earlier μ-feature correlations were Voellmy-μ; the conditions model trains on samosAT-μ and those results must be re-measured, not inherited.
  • The pre-harvest numerics gate. Stage 0 validated numerics at per-case optima; the harvest spends most evaluations off-optimum. Re-confirm the ordering there before harvesting.
  • The rule this campaign kept re-learning: measure, don’t argue. Seven times tonight a confident "this obviously can’t matter" or "this rule says adopt X" was checked by measurement, and several times the argument was wrong — the clip no-op, the numerics anchor, the ifa flatness prior, criterion 4’s operationalisation. The full seven-instance table, and the two operative rules it produced (reproduce a recommendation against its own response surface before freezing; when a surface and a single CI disagree, believe the surface), are in the plan doc.

The sequence from here

  • Harvest: per-event (μ, slab) calibration at the frozen vector across all ~1,500 clean cases, plus deliberately-random parameter draws to fix search-exhaust sampling bias. Hour-scale on a comparable fleet.
  • Per-dataset freezes and transfer tests, done rigorously (tonight's transfer stage is the first-pass version): 2019 first (same regime, isolates "different storm"), then 1999-dry vs 2018-dry (nuisance floor), then 1999-wet vs 1999-dry (the physics contrast). A pooled global-global vector ships only if transfer agrees; if it doesn't, the per-dataset vectors are the training signal.
  • The conditions model: deployable features only as the headline track; train-on-2018 → test-on-2019 out-of-storm generalisation is the experiment. The standing wall to beat: on deployable features alone, regression is currently indistinguishable from a global constant.
  • Later: emulator distillation, Hexagons/PowFinder integration, Austria/LAWIS extension.
